**Question:** Can an agent loop tune a classical grain segmentation pipeline so that grain count and mean grain size on optical micrographs match ground truth within 10%,
and how does accuracy degrade with image noise?

**Data checks** (script against the files, all 36 synthetic images with complete rows):
- There is one `labels_grain` image per name, shared by `clean/` and `corrupted/`.
  The CSV rows describe that ground truth.
  `corruption_level` and `artifact_area_fraction` describe the corrupted version.
- Labels tile the whole image (no boundary pixels), and `area_um2 = area_px × um_per_px²`.
- `n_grains` = rows in `ground_truth_grains.csv` for 36/36 images: it counts all grains, edge-touching ones included.
- `mean_eq_diameter_um` = mean `eq_diameter_um` of grains with `touches_edge == 0` for 36/36 images: interior grains only.
- The 12 `magset*` rows (1.0 and 2.0 µm/px) have empty mean columns and no rows in `ground_truth_grains.csv`.
- `corruption_level` takes the values 0.15, 0.35, 0.5, 0.55, 0.75 and 0.95 (the brief said up to 0.75).
- 316L masks hold only 0 and 1; there are 6–39 grains per image (median 19) on val and test.
  The images are a mix of `.png` and `.jpg`.

**Synthetic set (90_SYNTHETIC_exact_ground_truth): provenance unconfirmed,**
likely generated by an AI-written script on the day;
generator code not available, so the images are not redistributed.
Asked the scientist for the generator script (open question Q-1).
Answer: the set was generated with AI prompts and edited by hand; neither the prompts nor the code exist any more.
The dataset's SOURCES.md claims a `_generator_code` folder that does not exist.
Decision: the synthetic set is a tuning testbed, not scientific evidence;
the main claim is measured on the real 316L images (Warren et al. 2024, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.22039777, CC BY 4.0).

**Decisions:**
- **Split** (`data/splits.json`, seed 0):
  - synthetic: magset excluded; per family 4 dev / 2 holdout → 24 / 12;
  - 316L: `val` → dev (40), `test` → holdout (165), `train` unused.
- **Evaluator** (`src/evaluate.py`), like with like:
  - Count: all grains vs `n_grains`, or interior vs interior when the method drops edge grains.
  - Size: mean equivalent diameter of interior grains on both sides, px → µm with `um_per_px`.
  - 316L: grains are 4-connected components of mask = 1, sizes in px.
  - Main metric `max_rel_error` = max(median |count error|, median |size error|) over dev, target ≤ 0.10.
- **Method** `watershed`:
  - Pipeline: denoise → boundary map → distance-transform markers → watershed.
  - Boundary map: `threshold | sobel | tophat`.
    Tophat was added to the brief because some synthetic grains are as dark as the boundary lines, so a global threshold marks them as boundary.
  - Defaults were not tuned on the real data.
- **Tests** use a Voronoi micrograph built in code ([tests/voronoi.py](tests/voronoi.py)) instead of copying synthetic files of unknown licence.
  `data/sample/` holds two 316L train images with masks.
- **Overlays** are JPEG (quality 85, at most 1024 px wide), so a commit stays small.
  Label images are gitignored.
- **No `--dry-run --final` on the real data:** it would show holdout numbers before tuning.
  `--final` is tested on the Voronoi fixture only.

Confidence that the defaults do not meet 10%: high (>95%). Confidence in the failure diagnosis: about 80%.
- Next: Run a small grid on the synthetic dev split. Report per-morphology results as well as per-corruption results, and hold morphology fixed when describing noise trends.

1. **min_distance: 8 → {12, 16}.** True mean equivalent diameter is about 22–31 px, except fine_equiaxed at about 16 px. A spacing of 8 allows several markers per grain. Expect fine_equiaxed to start under-counting at 16, so watch that tradeoff.
2. **footprint: 15 → 25.** This suppresses secondary distance-transform maxima within a grain.
3. **sigma: 1.0 → 2.0.** This weakens thin scratches before the top-hat step. Also try **line_width_px: 4 → 3** if the boundaries in the ground truth are about 2–3 px wide. A narrower top-hat may still pick up scratches, so check the overlays.
4. **min_area_px: 40 → 80.** The smallest real grains are about 200 px², so this is safe and removes slivers and pore fragments.

State plainly: I expect min_distance and footprint alone cannot fix the columnar and elongated images. In grains with an aspect ratio around 4:1 and width around 15 px, the distance transform has a ridge with many peaks along its length. Any min_distance large enough to suppress them (more than about 40 px) will merge the fine equiaxed grains. If the grid leaves columnar or elongated count error above about 50%, change the marker method rather than the parameters, in one of two ways:
- **h-maxima markers** on the distance transform (h ≈ 2–3 px), which merge peaks along a ridge regardless of spacing.
- **Connected-component markers**, taken from grain interiors after thresholding the top-hat boundary map, with no distance-transform split.

Scratches crossing grains need a separate fix, because tuning thresholds will not remove straight lines. Either remove long straight boundary segments, for example a Hough or line-length filter on the boundary map before watershed, or require a minimum intensity contrast across each boundary.

Do not touch the holdout split, and do not test the real 316L images, until synthetic dev count error is below about 15% on most morphologies.

- Next: Change the decision metric before any further tuning, then replace the marker method; parameter tuning cannot fix the failure.

**1. Change the decision metric**
Score = max over families of median(|count_rel_error|, |size_rel_error|). Also report the 90th-percentile per-image error. Under this score the current run scores 0.514, not 0.076.

**2. Why no parameter of the existing watershed can fix it**
- columnar and elongated need wider marker spacing, roughly min_distance 14 -> ~22.
- fine_equiaxed needs narrower spacing; it already under-counts at 14, with grains about 16 px across.
- A single isotropic min_distance cannot satisfy both.

**3. New method: interior-component markers**
- Threshold the top-hat boundary response (Otsu or a fixed percentile).
- Take connected components of non-boundary pixels, after a 1–2 px erosion, as markers.
- Watershed on the boundary response.
- This follows the visible closed boundaries instead of distance-transform peaks, so elongated grains are not cut along their long axis.
- Fallback if boundaries are broken: h-maxima on the distance transform with h = 2–3 px instead of min_distance/footprint, with min_distance 14 -> 8 so h controls splitting.

**4. Scratch rejection**
Remove boundary segments that are straight (Hough or skeleton fit residual < 1.5 px) and long (> 80 px), and that cut through grain interiors. Or require mean intensity contrast across a boundary > k·σ_noise before merging regions.

**5. Pore and ring rejection**
Drop regions with circularity > 0.85 and area < 400 px², or regions whose mean intensity is far below their neighbours. Fill dark pore pixels into the surrounding grain.

**6. Keep everything else fixed**
Keep sigma=2, line_width_px=4 and min_area_px=80 so the change is attributable to the marker method. Evaluate per-family on dev.

**7. Noise sweep, only after all families are within 10%**
Render each family at all corruption levels with fixed geometry, so noise is not confounded with morphology. Leave the holdout and the real 316L images untouched until then.

## 2026-11-17 · Coarse cell structures: a twin family, a scoring CLI, and the private test's failures reproduced

**Why.** The tool was tested privately on optical micrographs of a coarse, elongated cell structure (8 images with hand-corrected masks; the images cannot be shared).
It lost to a plain h-maxima watershed on everything but mean direction: flake F1 0.64 vs 0.74 (IoU 0.5), S error 0.05–0.07 vs 0.02, median aspect ratio −22 % vs −12 %, one image 7° off in direction.

**The coarse-cell twin family** ([src/synth_cells.py](src/synth_cells.py), tests in [tests/test_synth_cells.py](tests/test_synth_cells.py)):
- Built from the description of the private images:

  - not cells: grey speckled pockets at triple junctions (0.5–2 cell widths), dark pores inside the cells;

### Fix 1 · Flake width from the spacing of the boundary lines
- *Hypothesis:* the Otsu-thickness estimate is pulled low by pores, pockets and broken boundaries. The spacing between boundary lines that run along the cells is not.
- *Change:* `boundary_spacing` in [src/methods/profile.py](src/methods/profile.py):
  - Sato ridges at 1–3 px, kept only where they run within 25° of the coarse local direction (pores, pocket texture and the transverse boundaries drop out);
  - 240 scan lines across that direction, with the spacing taken at the peak of the log-histogram of crossing distances (a missing boundary doubles one spacing: that moves the median, not the peak);
  - the mean intercept as a cross-check.

  The width becomes spacing − line width.
- *Rule* (to keep the calibrated behaviour elsewhere): the spacing estimate replaces the Otsu one only when the two disagree by more than 30 % **and** the spacing has a clear peak (≥ 40 % of spacings within ±25 %). Every profile reports both estimates, the intercept and a `disagree` flag.
- *Result:*
  - twin dev: 30.5 → **62.4 px** (true 55–75).
  - Real sets are unchanged, because the two estimates agree or the spacing has no clear peak.
    That holds for all 12 stress sets (on Ti64, spacing − line ≈ Otsu × 1.03–1.19).
    The only real image that switches is Ti64_29 (8.5 → 13.5 px).
  - Disagreements are flagged on SEM, basketweave, Widmanstätten and banded, which keep the old value.
- *Verdict:* kept. Tests: [tests/test_width.py](tests/test_width.py).

### Fix 2 · Pixel parameters capped by the image size
- *Hypothesis:* at 62 px, 20 × the width asks for scratches 1250 px long, longer than the image is tall, so none can be found.
- *Change:* `SIZE_CAPS` in `scaled_params`:
  - scratch length ≤ 0.35 × the short side (floor 40 px);
  - tile ≤ 0.5 × the short side;
  - flattening ≤ 0.25 × the long side;
  - gap bridging ≤ 0.05 × the short side.

  Given params are left alone.
- *Result:* fixes 1 + 2 together, twin dev F1 0.726 → **0.830**, pockets counted 542 → 131, aspect ratio bias −19 % → −10.6 %, S error 0.024 → 0.011.
- *Verdict:* kept. Tests in [tests/test_profile.py](tests/test_profile.py).

### Fix 4 · Junction pockets are "not a flake"
- *Hypothesis:* a pocket is fine speckle without a direction; boundary lines have a direction.
- *Tried first and rejected:*
  - band-pass texture energy, which is higher on the boundary lines than in the pockets;
  - energy plus coherence, which found 0 % of the pockets.
- *Change:* `textured_regions` in [src/methods/profile.py](src/methods/profile.py):
  - a density of small intensity extrema above 4× the image median and above 0.004 per pixel;
  - structure-tensor coherence below 0.3;
  - blobs of at least (0.2 w)²;
  - only for flakes ≥ 24 px wide (at finer scales speckle cannot be told from lamellae).

  Flakes lying at least half on a pocket are marked `not_a_flake`, left out of all statistics and of the label PNG, and drawn dark.
- *Result:* pockets counted 131 → **21**; F1 0.828 → **0.860**; aspect ratio bias −7.2 %; S error 0.006.
- *Verdict:* kept. Tests in [tests/test_flakes.py](tests/test_flakes.py) (pockets cut ≥ 70 %, cells found ≥ 97 %; never on fine lamellae).

### Fix 6 · Out-of-focus areas left out
- *Hypothesis:* in a blurred area the fine-scale gradient at the edges is weak relative to the coarse one.
- *What failed first:*
  - the sharpness ratio alone flagged 13 % of a sharp Ti64 image and 12–62 % of real images with broad step edges (Widmanstätten plates, ferrite grains);
  - a curvature-over-contrast index was swamped by camera noise.
- *Change:* `out_of_focus` in [src/methods/profile.py](src/methods/profile.py), applied in the region of interest. Two cues must hold together, relative to the image's own sharp part:
  - sharpness below 0.72 × the image's sharp part;
  - local contrast below 0.72 × its 75th percentile, since blur erases thin lines.

  The area must be at least 10 % of the image.
  The fraction left out is reported (`out_of_focus_fraction`).
- *Thresholds:* a scan was run on 8 twin images and on 47 real ones (Ti64, the lamellar set, the scientist's SEM image).
  The looser setting (0.8 × contrast) took a third of the scientist's SEM specimen for blurred.
  The chosen one flags **none of the 47 real images**, and removes 62–81 % of the blurred area in 7 of 8 twin images.
  One twin image is missed: the detector misses blur rather than cut sharp specimen away.
- *Verdict:* kept, conservative by design. Tests: blurred twin found (IoU ≥ 0.6), no flag on clean twin, laths or Ti64_21.

### Fix 3 · Over-splitting: pores filled, faint end-to-end boundaries kept
- *Diagnosis* (twin dev, after fixes 1, 2, 4, 6): 127 of 138 split cells were watershed cuts between touching pieces.
  In the drawings each cut runs from a pore to the boundary: the pore was a hole in the interior, and the distance-transform watershed cut the cell through it.
- *Change 1:* holes up to (0.15 × flake width)² are filled before the watershed (`_pore_area`; at least 11 px, the old fixed value, so nothing changes below ~22 px).
  - Result: splits 138 → 42, aspect ratio bias −7.2 % → **−1.2 %**.
  - But merges 88 → 155 (F1 0.86 → 0.85): some pore cuts had been splitting, by luck, cells that leaked into each other through gaps in their faint end-to-end boundary.
- *Tried for the merges* (12 dev images; each line is one run):

  | variant | F1 | merges / splits | aspect ratio bias | S error |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | pores filled only | 0.850 | 155 / 42 | −1.2 % | 0.004 |
  | + **border evidence** (kept) | **0.862** | 114 / 75 | −3.4 % | **0.004** |
  | + transverse bridging | 0.864 | 136 / 58 | −3.2 % | 0.008 |
  | + both | 0.865 | 106 / 99 | −5.2 % | 0.004 |
  | + both, direction field at half a flake width | 0.855 | 101 / 120 | −4.8 % | 0.016 |

  - *Border evidence* (`merge_border_darkness`, "auto" = 0.12 of the flake-to-boundary contrast for flakes ≥ 24 px): two pieces are not merged back when their shared border is darker than both. A cut inside one flake is not darker.
  - *Transverse bridging* (`bridge_ends_px`): faint lines across the flakes taken at a lower threshold and closed along their own direction. It trades merges for splits; available, default off.
  - A *wider direction field* made merges worse: with the direction read correctly, the merge rule joins end-to-end neighbours more often. The narrow field had been blocking those merges by accident.
- *Verdict:* pore filling and border evidence kept. The differences between the variants (±1.5 F1 points) are within what 12 images can resolve.
- Tests: pores (splits ≤ 7 % of cells, aspect ratio bias > −8 %), and a unit test of the border evidence (a darker shared border keeps two pieces apart; a plain one merges).

### Fix 5 · Scratches on coarse cells
- *Before:* recall 0 on the twin. At the old scaled length (1250 px) no scratch could exist; with the cap (fix 2) still none was found.
- *Diagnosis* with the scratch finder's own log:
  - 18 % of the scratch pixels reached the candidate map, because the ridge threshold is set by the strong cell boundaries;
  - inside 60 px cells the 8-px direction field is noise, so the "same direction on both sides" test failed;
  - the contrast test sampled 3 px to the side, inside a thick scratch.
- *Changes,* for flakes ≥ 24 px only (`COARSE_PX`; the Ti64 scratch behaviour is unchanged):
  - the scratch tests and the infill use a direction field at half a flake width, with the sides sampled 0.8 flake widths away (closer, a thick scratch pulled the direction towards itself);
  - candidates at 0.4 × the ridge threshold, plus a dark pass at 2–3 px for thick scratches;
  - contrast sampled 3 and 6 px to either side, against max(3 × pixel noise, half the usual level);
  - the minimum crossing angle is 25°: rows curve, and their long boundaries ran 16–26° off the smoothed direction and were taken for scratches;
  - the contrast fraction is 0.45: chains of aligned faint end-to-end boundaries across the cells were dark along only 30–39 % of their length, real scratches along 55–78 %;
  - pockets are found before the scratches, so their texture does not feed candidates;
  - the minimum length cap is 0.25 × the short side, never below the reference default of 200 px. A lower cap on small images of fine flakes let short false lines through: a pasted-scratch test failed.
- *Result* (12 dev images):
  - crossing-scratch recall, median per image: 0 → **0.46**;
  - precision 0.88; **0 false scratch pixels on the 4 clean images**;
  - lamellar_synth scratch precision/recall 0.917/0.811 (was 0.920/0.818).
- *What is still missed:* scratches within ~25° of the local cell direction (on dev, the thick scratch of scratched_0 at 10°, one at 24° in a curved row), and the faintest bright hairlines.
- Tests: hairline and thick scratches across coarse cells (≥ 40 % of the crossing pixels, ≤ 20 % false), none on clean coarse cells.

### A calibration card ([src/calibration_card.py](src/calibration_card.py))
- *Hypothesis:* a fixed set of statistics measured from images and masks (numbers only) is enough to fit a twin to images that cannot leave their owner.
- *What it measures,* per image (formulas in the module docstring):
  - interior level (median grey of the cells eroded by 3 px);
  - number of cells, and the median minor axis, major axis and aspect ratio of the cells that touch neither the image edge nor a pocket or ignore region;
  - mean direction and S (as `src.score_masks`);
  - boundary contrast per boundary pixel, (I − m) / I with m the darkest boundary pixel in a 5 × 5 window, as p25 / median / p75;
  - the invisible share (contrast < 0.05), along and across the cells (from the structure tensor of the cell indicator);
  - the boundary share of the image; noise (std of g − gaussian(g, 3) in cells eroded by 10 px, without dots, / I);
  - dark dots per megapixel; pocket and ignore shares; illumination range (p95 − p5 of gaussian(g, 100), / median);
  - sharpness: |Laplacian of gaussian(g, 1)| in 128 px tiles, median and p10, relative to the sharpest image of the set.
- A card summarises [min, max] over the images (sharp images for contrast, invisible shares and sharpness; soft ones, ignore share ≥ 10 %, separately).
- `python -m src.calibration_card --images DIR --masks DIR --out card.json [--scale 0.5]`; the private numbers ship as [data/cards/coarse_cells_example.json](data/cards/coarse_cells_example.json) (written by hand from the scientist's message, not computed by the tool).
- Tests ([tests/test_calibration_card.py](tests/test_calibration_card.py)): known answers on drawn bricks (widths by second moments, contrast, invisible gaps along but not across, noise, dots, pockets, ignore, illumination), sharpness relative to the sharpest image, the CLI's pairing, the example card's completeness.
- *Verdict:* done. The private side can now compute the card with the same formulas the twin is judged by.

### A calibrated generator ([src/synth_cells.py](src/synth_cells.py); the first one kept as [src/synth_cells_v1.py](src/synth_cells_v1.py))
- *Hypothesis:* a generator drawn from the card's numbers reproduces the private failures.
- *What it draws:*
  - geometry: an anisotropic, multiplicatively weighted Voronoi tessellation of seeds in staggered, curved rows: the field is filled with cells like flattened pebbles (tapered or rounded ends, widths varying ~2× within an image, some curved boundaries, cells cut by the edge), no matrix;
  - near-white flat interiors (0.96-0.99), faint beaded boundaries (per-segment contrast, bead modulation along the line, gaps where the line vanishes), transverse boundaries as visible as longitudinal ones;
  - elongated grey inclusions lying in the boundaries, speckled pockets at triple junctions, pores and a few black blobs, a fine speckle;
  - hairline scratches (~45° to the cells), thick dark bars at any angle (optionally ~20°), short debris marks;
  - weak illumination, noise correlated over ~1.8 px (as after JPEG and downscaling), 8 bits and a mild JPEG;
  - soft images: a focus gradient across the field (a tilted specimen), from a softer best side (σ 1.9 px) through a readable band to an unreadable part (σ ≥ 3 px, "ignore" in the truth, and every cell touching it).
- The first generator stays as `src/synth_cells_v1.py` for the tests and results made with it.
- *Calibration* (several rounds against the card; [tests/test_synth_cells.py](tests/test_synth_cells.py)):

  - the boundary share is 6-7 % against ~10 %. The card is not consistent with itself here: ~100 cells of ~60 × 180 px with 2-3 px boundaries give ~5 %; the card tool will compute all numbers consistently on the private side;

Twelve random twin images spread more than eight real ones on cell-population medians (aspect ratio up to 3.5, up to 166 cells); recorded here because it relaxes a test written before the code.

### Fix 4 · Width from the fundamental spacing of a hysteresis line map
- *Hypothesis:* the private profile read 34 px because the Otsu thickness is pulled down by dots, pockets and broken lines, and the line-spacing estimator never took over (its peak was never clear enough).

### Fix 6 · Soft regions from the width of the boundary lines
